Long-form research behind Nah?. These are working documents, not marketing. They
contain the numbers that argue against the project as well as the ones that argue
for it.

Figures are labelled by origin throughout. A company claim is what a company says
about itself. An estimate comes from an outside tracker and is modelled rather
than measured. Where something could not be verified, the documents say so.

## The documents

### [Concept audit: Nah? ten months in]({{ '/research/concept-audit/' | relative_url }})

*11 September 2026.* Where the project actually stands, where the specs and the
decision records contradict each other, who else is in this space, whether one
person can build it, and a server architecture designed so any family could run
its own.

### [What makes people come back]({{ '/research/what-makes-people-come-back/' | relative_url }})

*12 September 2026.* How the private-social apps are really doing, which return
mechanics work, why BeReal collapsed, what the research says about habits and
notifications, whether anyone pays, and why the calm ones keep dying.

### [Circles and the backend question]({{ '/research/circles-and-the-backend/' | relative_url }})

*13 September 2026.* A person belongs to many overlapping circles at once. Can
Mastodon, the wider fediverse or Matrix express that? What per-circle digests,
dying circles and archives should look like.

### [Spike results: which runtime, and how many databases]({{ '/research/spike-results/' | relative_url }})

*13 September 2026.* Four candidate servers measured against thresholds written
in advance, plus the SQLite topology question and the digest storm. The result
overturned the framework choice.
